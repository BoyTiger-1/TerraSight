About TerraSight

Built because the smoke reached our street.

The story

Two years ago a wildfire broke out an hour from my community. For days the air was thick with smoke, evacuation alerts loomed, and my family packed bags we hoped we would not need. We were lucky. The LA fires that followed showed how many people are not.

I started with a single wildfire prediction model. It worked, but the more I learned, the clearer it became that no hazard exists alone: the drought feeds the fire, the fire poisons the air, the burn scar sheds the debris flow. So the one model became sixteen coordinated modules, and the demo data became real satellite feeds, reanalysis archives, river gauges, and seismic catalogs.

TerraSight is what I wished existed when we were watching that smoke column: one place that says what is likely, why, how confident it is, and what to do.

Design principles
Real data or no data.
Every number traces to NASA, NOAA, USGS, or Open-Meteo. The training sets are documented historical disasters matched with ERA5 reanalysis, and each module lists its sources.
Explainable, or it does not ship.
Each prediction shows the variables that drove it, a confidence label, and a model card with cross-validated performance.
Honest about the limits of science.
Earthquakes and eruptions cannot be predicted. Those modules model impact and readiness instead, and say so on the page.
Physics beats overfitting.
The ML models are monotonically constrained: more wind can never mean less fire, more rain can never mean more drought.
Show your work, including the failures.
The validation page publishes cross-validated ROC and precision-recall curves, calibration diagrams, the confusion matrix at the cutoff the product actually uses, and the twelve events each model got most wrong. A model nobody has audited is an opinion with decimal places.
Never "no data available".
Some feeds genuinely do not exist at every point on earth: a river gauge needs a river, sea surface temperature needs ocean. So each feed walks outward until it finds a real reading, a throttled API serves its last good response instead of an empty panel, and any substitution is labelled with the distance it came from rather than passed off as local.
Technology
LayerWhat runs there
Machine learningGradient-boosted classifiers (scikit-learn) trained on 80 documented US wildfires and 37 US floods with ERA5 features, monotonic physics constraints, occlusion-based local explanations
Science modelsSPI-style drought ranking, percentile heatwave detection, Caine intensity-duration landslide thresholds, Gutenberg-Richter seismicity, Omori aftershock decay, Rankine cyclone wind decay, FAO-56 water balance
Data feedsOpen-Meteo (forecast, ERA5 archive, air quality, GloFAS flood, CMIP6, marine, elevation), USGS (earthquakes, river gauges, volcanoes), NOAA (NWS alerts, NHC storms), NASA (EONET, GIBS, FIRMS)
National pipelineA land-masked grid of roughly 800 points covering all 50 states, DC, and Puerto Rico, fetched in bulk multi-coordinate requests, scored by the same models the module pages use, and refreshed on a budget-aware schedule so the hazard field covers the whole country rather than only where an event was reported
Seven day outlookEvery grid point is scored on each of the next seven days, not just today, so the national map animates forward through the week. The forecast window each lead day sees is held fixed, otherwise the map would appear to calm down at the end of the week purely because the forecast ran out
Watchlist and alertsSaved locations are checked against the grid's week-ahead peak rather than only today's score, and a national feed diffs consecutive builds to report what crossed a risk band. The watchlist lives in the browser, so there are no accounts and nothing about who is watching what is stored on the server
ValidationOut-of-fold cross-validation over the persisted training rows: ROC, precision-recall, reliability diagrams, Brier skill against the base rate, and the full threshold tradeoff table, regenerated on every retrain
BackendPython, Flask, cached data layer with stale-while-error fallback, 16 module engines plus cascade, scenario, report, and economics services
FrontendVanilla JavaScript, Leaflet with NASA GIBS satellite layers, Chart.js, a single design system
