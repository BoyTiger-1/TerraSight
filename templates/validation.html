Model validation

Does it actually work?

Every risk score on this platform comes from a model, and a model that nobody has audited is just an opinion with decimal places. This page is the audit. Every number below comes from out-of-fold cross-validation: the data is split five ways, and each row is scored by a model that never saw it during training, so nothing here is the model grading its own homework.

Two questions matter, and they are different. Discrimination asks whether the model ranks a real event above a quiet day. Calibration asks whether a 70 means seventy percent. A model can be excellent at the first and useless at the second, and only the second makes the number safe to read literally.

How to read this, and what it does not tell you

ROC AUC is the chance the model scores a randomly chosen event day above a randomly chosen non-event day. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. The per-fold spread next to it matters as much as the average: a tight spread means the number is real, a wide one means the dataset is small enough that luck moves it.

Average precision is the honest headline when events are the minority class. ROC flatters a model on imbalanced data; the precision-recall curve does not.

Brier skill compares the squared error of the predicted probabilities against the base rate. Positive is better than always guessing the average; zero means the model has added nothing.

The reliability diagram bins predictions and plots what actually happened in each bin. Points on the diagonal mean the probabilities can be read at face value. Points below it mean the model is overconfident. ECE is the average distance from that diagonal, weighted by how many rows land in each bin.

What this does not tell you: these datasets are built from historical event records paired with reanalysis weather, sampled over the continental United States. Performance on a hazard type, region, or season that is thinly represented here is not measured by these numbers, and a model that scores well on cross-validation can still be wrong about a place it has barely seen. The scores on this platform are decision support, not a warning service.
